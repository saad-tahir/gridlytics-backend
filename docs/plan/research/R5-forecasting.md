# R5 - Forecasting stack for the OnyxLeaf EMS (PV, load, outage)

Written 6 November 2026 by the R5-forecasting research agent. Self-contained notes for the build plan.
Scope: three forecasters at 15-minute resolution over a 48-hour horizon with quantiles, re-issued every 15 minutes, feeding a MILP/MPC optimiser (HiGHS) for Pakistani C&I sites with PV + LFP battery + unreliable grid + diesel genset.

Conventions: every claim carries a source URL and the date it was checked (6 Nov 2026 unless stated). Anything not confirmed from a primary source is marked UNVERIFIED. Prices are as printed on the vendor page on the day checked; exchange rates are not applied.

Inputs already decided by the guideline documents (C:/Users/SaadTahir/Videos/solarytics/Perfect-EMS_TOU_Dispatch_Deep_Review.md lines 175-179 and "Product 1 - TOU-Aware Energy Management and Dispatch.md" lines 382-393, 455, 501, 567):
- PV: pvlib clear-sky x learned attenuation on day 0, then a GBM on NWP features after 4-6 weeks of telemetry; Quartz is a benchmark only (UK-trained).
- Load: gradient-boosted trees on calendar, lag and weather features; load history is censored during outages.
- Outage: DISCO schedule as input, plus a learned per-feeder hazard by hour of day, season and recent history; every deployed site is a sensor.
- WP5 acceptance: PV daytime skill >= 25% vs smart persistence over 4 weeks; load beats seasonal persistence; outage model beats schedule-only. Metrics: daytime nRMSE, skill vs smart persistence, pinball loss, Brier score.
- Installed Python (conda env ops, 3.12): numpy 2.4, scipy 1.17, pandas 3.0, scikit-learn 1.8, xgboost 3.2, fastapi 0.141. NOT installed: highspy, cvxpy, pulp, pyomo, pvlib, skforecast, lightgbm.

### 1.2 XGBoost quantile regression (installed xgboost 3.2)
Parameter page (https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/parameter.html): `reg:quantileerror` = "Quantile loss, also known as pinball loss. A smooth approximation is used to optimize the quantile loss." (added in 2.0.0). `quantile_alpha` = "A scalar or an ascending list of targeted quantiles." So one booster emits all quantiles (output shape n x n_quantiles); crossing is not prevented (the page notes only `reg:expectileerror` "does not suffer from curve crossing"), so sort quantiles after prediction (OpenSTEF does this with its `QuantileSorter`). scikit-learn 1.8 (installed) also offers `HistGradientBoostingRegressor(loss="quantile", quantile=q)`, one model per quantile (known API, not re-fetched).

## 3. Pakistani data: irradiance, soiling, load, outage schedules (checked 6 Nov 2026)

### 3.1 Irradiance and soiling
- World Bank / ESMAP "Pakistan - Solar Radiation Measurement Data": 9 automated stations (Bahawalpur/Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park, Hyderabad, Islamabad, Karachi, Khuzdar, Lahore, Multan, Peshawar and one more), 10-minute averages of GHI/DNI/DHI, temperature, pressure, wind; campaign 2015-2017; CC BY 4.0; mirrored at https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0038550/pakistan-solar-radiation-measurement-data and https://energydata.info/dataset/pakistan-solar-radiation-measurement-data (energydata.info returned HTTP 403 to this agent; station list and dates are from the catalogue search snippets: partly UNVERIFIED). Use: calibrate the day-0 clear-sky/Linke-turbidity priors and the seasonal clear-sky-index climatology per city; validate the Open-Meteo radiation bias per month before any site exists.
- Satellite vs ground bias, Peshawar (Ayaz et al., Energies 15(7):2528, 2022, https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jeners/v15y2022i7p2528-d783019.html): satellite (SUNY) GHI exceeded ground GHI by up to "42.90% ... in the month of December" and DNI by 55.86% in November; ground exceeded satellite only in Feb-Apr (-3.83% GHI in March); R2 0.885 GHI, 0.414 DNI. This is the winter smog/haze over-prediction the guideline infers; a per-site, per-month bias correction (Stage 1 below) is mandatory, and DNI from any provider must be treated as untrustworthy in Nov-Jan.
- Soiling (Lahore): Ullah et al. 2019 (Renewable Energy 139:830-843) 26.2% energy loss on a horizontal panel after a 100-day dust exposure vs 13.5% vertical; Ullah et al. 2020 (Renewable Energy 150:456-468, cited in the Product 1 report as ~0.8%/day at 30 deg tilt, weekly cleaning optimal). Sargodha (Farhan et al. 2022, Frontiers in Energy Research): 7.3-10.7% monthly loss near stone crushers. Implication: a soiling state (days since last rain >1 mm or logged cleaning, capped loss ~25%) is a first-class feature and the clear-day performance ratio must be re-estimated weekly, not assumed constant.
- No public 2023-2026 Pakistani study of NWP/satellite irradiance bias in smog season was found (searched 6 Nov 2026): the fleet's own telemetry will be the first such dataset; log it as a product asset.

### 3.2 Load datasets
- PRECON (LUMS Energy Informatics Group, Nadeem and Arshad, ACM e-Energy 2019): 42 Lahore homes, one year, 1-minute whole-house and appliance-level data, CC0 on https://opendata.com.pk/dataset/precon-pakistan-residential-electricity-consumption-dataset (paper https://web.lums.edu.pk/~eig/pdf/PRECON.pdf). Residential, so only useful for (a) testing the pipeline end to end before pilot data exists, (b) Ramadan/Eid/heat-wave calendar effects in Pakistani demand. No public Pakistani C&I 15-minute load dataset was found; C&I day-0 profiles must come from templates (section 4.2).

### 3.3 Load-shedding schedules (outage prior inputs)
- Legal basis: on the Pakistan Information Commission's orders under the Right of Access to Information Act 2017, the Power Division directed all DISCOs to upload feeder-wise load-shedding schedules and real-time outage information on their websites; APP reported compliance starting 31 March 2026 (https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1186294, https://rti.gov.pk/NewsDetail/ODRiM2E5ZmQtYmQ2OC00NmI5LWJiNzItNzM1ZTY5MWFlYzU5). A separate 14 April 2026 "Peak Relief Strategy" imposed up to 2.25 h/day of 5 pm-1 am load shedding with feeder-wise schedules shared with consumers; K-Electric and HESCO were excluded (https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/04/14/5pm-1am-peak-load-shedding-govt-announces-up-to-2-25-hour-daily-outages/).
- Ex-WAPDA DISCOs (LESCO, IESCO, GEPCO, FESCO, MEPCO, PESCO, HESCO, SEPCO, QESCO, TESCO, HAZECO): the central system is PITC's CCMS. https://ccms.pitc.com.pk/FeederDetails takes a "14-digit reference number" or "6-digit feeder code" and shows "Feeder:", "Grid:", "Feeder Status:", "Net Metering Capacity:", "Sub Division:", a load-management hours readout and a "Feeder ON/OFF Status" timeline ("Loading Feeder ON/OFF Timeline...", "Fetching live feeder records..."). https://ccms.pitc.com.pk/flsfeeder_index searches by "Feeder Code", "Feeder Name" or "Grid Name" and returns "Feeder Code, Feeder Name, City, Grid Station, Disco". The Roshan Pakistan portal (https://www.roshanpakistan.pk/, Ministry of Energy/PITC) links to the same CCMS pages and to "LMR Daily" at https://lmr.pitc.com.pk/ (returned HTTP 503 on 6 Nov 2026: UNVERIFIED content). No JSON API or bulk download is documented; the data loads dynamically after a search, so the integration is a polite scraper (one request per feeder per hour, cached) plus manual entry in the site onboarding form. Treat scraped text as untrusted data; parse strictly.
- K-Electric (Karachi, not in CCMS): publishes feeder-wise PDF schedules on ke.com.pk (e.g. https://ke.com.pk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Load-Shed-Schedule-20-Oct-2025.pdf, 9 pages, feeder names grouped by IBC/area such as "Landhi KEPZ"), the "KE Live" app ("Power" tab shows the feeder name), a WhatsApp self-service portal, and SMS alerts ("REGISTER" to 8119). Load shedding is set per feeder by loss category; October 2026 review: ~70% of ~2,100 feeders exempt, but ARY reported shedding on 150+ previously exempt feeders on 24 Oct 2026. Integration: PDF-table parser per release + manual entry; no API.
- Third-party apps (checkbills.pk, mepcobillonline.com, thepakistanconnect.com) republish schedules; unofficial, no API, do not depend on them.
- Design consequence: the schedule is a weak, often-violated prior (DISCOs deny "forced load-shedding" while consumers report unscheduled cuts; the NA Question Hour of Feb 2026 counted 2,000+ feeders with >10 h/day). The learned feeder hazard from fleet telemetry is the real forecaster; the schedule is one feature.

## 4. Recommended pipeline per forecaster

Common conventions: all series in UTC internally, displayed in Asia/Karachi (no DST); 15-minute left-labelled slots; every forecast row carries `belief_time` (issue time), `target_time`, `horizon_min`, quantiles q05, q10, q25, q50, q75, q90, q95, `model_stage`, `nwp_run_time`, `nwp_source`. Quantiles are sorted after prediction. Stages are per site and advance automatically on data-sufficiency gates, never on calendar time alone.

### 4.1 PV forecaster (per site)
Stage 0, day 0 (no telemetry): physics only.
1. Site record: lat/lon, kWp DC per plane, tilt, azimuth, inverter kW AC, module temperature coefficient (default -0.0035/K), mounting (roof/ground), commissioning date, cleaning routine. Horizon profile from PVGIS `printhorizon`; TMY and monthly yields from PVGIS `seriescalc` (ERA5 if SARAH3 fails) and NASA POWER climatology.
2. NWP pull per grid cell from Open-Meteo: hourly shortwave_radiation, direct_radiation, diffuse_radiation, direct_normal_irradiance, temperature_2m, relative_humidity_2m, wind_speed_10m, cloud_cover_low/mid/high, precipitation (10 variables = 1 call), forecast_days=3, models=best_match plus a second pull with models=ecmwf_ifs025 for diversity; air quality aerosol_optical_depth, dust, pm2_5 4x/day.
3. 15-minute downscale: compute hourly clear-sky index kt = GHI_nwp / GHI_clearsky(ineichen, hour mean); interpolate kt linearly to 15-min; multiply by the 15-min clear-sky GHI. Same for the diffuse fraction. This avoids sunrise/sunset artefacts that plain interpolation of hourly means creates. Decompose with `erbs_driesse` only when the provider's direct/diffuse split is missing.
4. Transposition `get_total_irradiance(model="perez-driesse")` per plane, cell temperature `pvsyst_cell` (or `faiman`), `pvwatts_dc` with gamma_pdc, `pvwatts_losses` with soiling set from the soiling state (default 3% after rain, +0.5%/dry day capped at 20% in Oct-Feb, +0.3%/day otherwise: own defaults derived from the Lahore studies, to be replaced by learned values), `inverter.pvwatts` with AC clipping, then horizon shading mask from PVGIS.
5. Quantiles on day 0: multiplicative error bands by horizon and kt bin from a regional table seeded with Solcast's published arid/humid nRMSE (16.7% / 34.8% at +24 h) and NWP-vs-ESMAP-station residuals computed offline (one-off study on the 2015-2017 stations using the Open-Meteo Historical Forecast archive, which starts 2017 for IFS HRES only: use ERA5 as a proxy and mark the table "prior").
Stage 1, from day 3 (after ~3 daylight days of 1-min telemetry):
6. Capacity and bias fit: on `detect_clearsky` periods, fit P_meas = a * P_phys (robust Huber regression) -> effective capacity a (captures nameplate error, orientation error, soiling, degradation); refit nightly with a 14-day window and expose a as "performance ratio" in the UI (cleaning alerts when a drops >8% below its 30-day max without rain).
7. Residual quantiles: conformal intervals from the last 14-28 days of residuals, binned by horizon class (0-2 h, 2-6 h, 6-24 h, 24-48 h) and by kt class (clear/intermediate/overcast). This already beats the day-0 bands.
8. Intraday blend for 0-2 h: ratio r = mean(P_meas / P_fcst over the last 30 min); forecast(t+h) = P_fcst(t+h) * (1 + (r-1) * exp(-h / 90 min)). This is the "smart persistence" component and also the baseline we score against.
Stage 2, from week 4-6 (gate: >= 1,500 daytime 15-min samples and >= 10 non-clear days):
9. Gradient-boosted residual model: xgboost `reg:quantileerror`, `quantile_alpha=[0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9,0.95]`, target y = P_meas / P_phys_clearsky (clear-sky-normalised power, size-invariant), features: P_phys/P_clearsky (the Stage-1 output), kt_nwp, the Quartz nine NWP variables, DNI/DHI split, AOD, dust, PM2.5, relative humidity, month, hour, solar elevation and azimuth, days_since_rain, days_since_cleaning, a_ratio (Stage-1 capacity), lead time, NWP model id, and for h <= 6 h the latest observed kt and its 1-h trend. Train on features from the Historical Forecast / Previous Runs archives at the matching lead time, never on reanalysis. Monotone constraint on kt_nwp. ~2,000 trees, depth 6, learning rate 0.03, early stopping on the last 7 days.
10. Fleet model from month 3: one model over all sites with site features (city, mounting, tilt, cleaning regime, PR) plus a per-site residual correction (Stage-1 style); new sites start on the fleet model at Stage 0.5 instead of pure physics once >= 20 sites exist in the same NWP region. Regime handling is implicit (month + AOD + humidity + precipitation features); add explicit `regime` = {dry, monsoon (Jul-Sep), smog (Nov-Jan)} as a categorical to speed learning.
Outputs: 192 slots x 7 quantiles of AC kW, plus the clear-sky ceiling per slot (the optimiser uses it as an upper bound).

### 4.2 Load forecaster (per site)
Stage 0, day 0: template profile. Onboarding form: site type (office, retail, 1/2/3-shift industrial, cold store, telecom tower, hospital, school), operating hours per weekday, weekly off day(s), contracted/MDI kW, main process loads, HVAC share, Ramadan behaviour (daytime shutdown? night shift?). A template library of 15-min weekly profiles per type (normalised to MDI; seeded from engineering judgement and from the pilot sites; the PRECON data only helps for residential and calendar effects). Quantiles: template spread +/- 25% (q10/q90) on day 0.
Stage 1, from day 3: equivalent-day persistence. Forecast = median of the same slot on the last k same-day-type days (k=3 then 7), scaled by the ratio of the last 2 h observed to the same-day-type median (decays with horizon as in PV). Day type = {working, half-day (Friday/Saturday as configured), off-day, public holiday (holidays PK), Ramadan working day, Eid}. Quantiles from the empirical spread across the k days plus conformal residuals after week 2. This is also the "seasonal persistence" baseline that WP5 says the ML must beat.
Stage 2, from week 4 (gate: >= 3 weeks of 15-min data with < 10% gaps, >= 1 off-day cycle): direct multi-horizon GBM, one xgboost model with `horizon_min` as a feature (OpenSTEF pattern) and lags restricted to what is available at belief time (versioned lags: lag_h..lag_h+2h at 15-min, lag_24h, lag_48h, lag_168h, rolling 1 h / 24 h / 7 d means and maxima), calendar (hour sin/cos, day-of-week, day type, Ramadan flag and day-of-Ramadan, days to/after Eid, month), weather (temperature_2m, apparent temperature, relative humidity, cloud cover, with the same lead-time-faithful archive for training), site operations (planned shutdown flags entered by users, shift calendar), and tariff-time flags (peak window) because owners already shift load. Quantiles q05..q95 via `quantile_alpha` list; sort; clip at [0, 1.3 x MDI].
Censoring during outages (required by the brief): the training target is site load = PV_ac + battery_discharge + grid_import + genset_output - battery_charge - export, reconstructed from 1-min inverter telemetry (not grid import). A sample is marked `censored=1` when any of: grid absent and backup supply limited (genset off and SOC <= inverter cut-off + 5 points), the inverter reports overload/derating, or load fell > 40% within 2 minutes of a grid-loss event and stayed down until grid return. Censored samples are excluded from the GBM training set and from persistence medians (they are lower bounds, not observations); they are kept with the flag for a separate "critical load" estimate = the 75th percentile of observed load during outages when backup was adequate, per hour of day, which the optimiser's reserve constraint consumes. If censoring exceeds 30% of samples on a feeder, fall back to the template for the affected hours and surface a data-quality warning.
Stage 3 (later): fleet-level hierarchical model (site embedding by type) and Chronos/foundation-model zero-shot as a cold-start alternative (BuildingsBench/Chronos: UNVERIFIED licences this session; keep out of v1).

### 4.3 Outage probability forecaster (per feeder)
Inputs: feeder id (6-digit PITC code or KE feeder name, captured at onboarding from the bill), DISCO, the scraped/manual schedule (slot-level "scheduled off" indicator for the next 48 h), 1-minute grid-present flags from every site on the feeder (pooled: all sites on a feeder are sensors), calendar (hour, day-of-week, month/season, Ramadan, Eid, national holidays), weather forecast (temperature_2m max, heat index, thunderstorm/precipitation flag: demand-driven shedding and faults), recent history (outages in last 24 h / 7 d, hours since last outage, schedule adherence over the last 7 days), optional manual national-shortfall level (NPCC/press announcements, 0-3).
Model, discrete-time hazard at 15-min slots, two components combined as P(down_t) = 1 - (1 - p_sched_t)(1 - p_unsched_t):
- p_sched_t = schedule indicator x adherence rate. Adherence is a Beta-Binomial per feeder (prior Beta(8,2) = 80% adherence, updated per scheduled slot observed), so day 0 uses the prior and the fleet average, and it converges within ~20 scheduled slots (2-3 days on a 4-cycle feeder). Separate adherence for "starts on time" and "ends on time" (+/-1 slot tolerance), because late restoration is the costly error.
- p_unsched_t = logistic regression (sklearn) on hour-of-day (24 one-hot), season, day type, heat index forecast, precipitation flag, recent-history features, DISCO and feeder loss category; hierarchical cold start: DISCO-level coefficients as the prior (fitted on the fleet), feeder-specific intercept with L2 shrinkage; refit weekly; replace by a small GBM (`binary:logistic`) once a feeder has >= 60 unscheduled events.
- Duration model: empirical survival curve of outage length per feeder (separate for scheduled and unscheduled; lognormal fit until 30 events), used (a) to extend P(down) across subsequent slots as a hazard chain, and (b) at run time, when an outage is in progress, to give P(still down at t+k) from the conditional survival.
- Scenario output for the optimiser: besides per-slot P(down), sample K=20 outage paths (start slot and duration) from the hazard chain so a later stochastic MPC can use them; v1 optimiser uses P(down) thresholds and the reserve rule.
- Real-time hooks: an observed grid loss re-issues the outage forecast immediately and triggers the optimiser event re-solve (guideline line 170).
Cold start without a schedule (feeder not found in CCMS): the DISCO-level hourly climatology of unscheduled outages from the fleet, or if the fleet is empty, a hand-entered prior (e.g., QESCO/PESCO/SEPCO feeders: 10 h/day; LESCO/IESCO urban: 2 h/day) flagged "prior" in the UI.

## 5. Training and inference cadence

| Job | Cadence | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| nwp_fetch | hourly at :05 (deterministic), 4x/day for ensemble and air quality | per NWP grid cell | Cache in `nwp_runs` keyed by (cell, model, run_time); retries with exponential backoff; staleness flag after 3 h |
| schedule_fetch | hourly | per feeder | CCMS scraper + KE PDF watcher; diff -> `outage_schedule_versions` |
| forecast_issue | every 15 min at :01, after telemetry ingest, before the optimiser | all sites | PV, load, outage; ~10-30 ms per site for GBM inference, batched by cell; event-triggered re-issue on outage detected, genset start, PV bust (abs error > 30% of clear-sky over 30 min), plan rejection |
| stage1_refit | nightly 01:30 PKT | per site | capacity/bias ratio, soiling state, conformal residual tables, persistence baselines |
| site_model_train | nightly 02:00 PKT for sites in Stage 2 (incremental, last 90 days) and weekly full retrain | per site | xgboost CPU, < 1 min per site; checkpoint to object storage with model_version |
| fleet_model_train | weekly (Sunday 03:00 PKT) | all sites | PV and load fleet models; promotion only if backtest pinball loss improves on the last 14 days |
| outage_update | every slot for Beta-Binomial counts (trivial), weekly logistic/GBM refit | per feeder/DISCO | |
| scoring | daily 04:00 PKT | all sites | scores yesterday's forecasts at every belief time and horizon; writes `forecast_scores`; raises alerts on skill regression |
| backfill | at onboarding | per site/cell | 2 years of Historical Forecast features + PVGIS/NASA priors; one-off ESMAP-station bias study at platform start |

Compute: at 1,000 sites, issue = 1,000 x 3 models x 192 rows every 15 min = well under one CPU-minute; training = 1,000 x ~40 s nightly = ~11 CPU-hours spread over the night on 2-4 workers. No GPU.

## 6. Evaluation metrics (pre-registered, computed per site, horizon class, month and regime)
- PV daytime nRMSE: RMSE over slots with solar elevation > 5 deg, divided by AC nameplate kW (also report the version normalised by mean daytime power, for comparison with Solcast). Target: <= 12% at 24 h ahead in the dry season by Stage 2.
- Skill vs smart persistence: SS = 1 - RMSE_model / RMSE_sp, where smart persistence = clear-sky profile x the clear-sky index observed over the last hour (intraday) or the same hours yesterday (day-ahead). WP5 gate: >= 25% over 4 weeks. Also report MAE % of capacity, excluding night, for the Quartz comparison (13%).
- Load: nMAE and nRMSE normalised by mean load; skill vs equivalent-day persistence (last week same slot, same day type); WP5 gate: beat it at every horizon class.
- Probabilistic: pinball loss per quantile averaged over the seven quantiles; CRPS approximated from the quantile set; interval coverage of q10-q90 (target 80% +/- 5 points) and q05-q95 (90%); reliability by regime; PIT histograms monthly.
- Outage: Brier score per slot vs two baselines: schedule-only (p = adherence prior inside scheduled slots, base rate elsewhere) and climatology (feeder hourly base rate); Brier skill score; reliability diagram; event-level recall and precision of outage starts within +/-1 slot at P > 0.5; mean absolute error of predicted outage duration. WP5 gate: beat schedule-only.
- Economic: forecast-driven plan cost vs perfect-foresight plan cost (regret, PKR/day) from the counterfactual replay, reported separately so forecast error is visible in rupees (the NREL finding that forecast error can erase MPC value is the risk this metric watches).
- Data quality counters: telemetry completeness, censored share, NWP staleness, schedule availability.

## 7. Storage and serving
- `nwp_runs` (hypertable or partitioned table): cell_id, model, run_time, valid_time, variable columns; keep 2 years (training features), compress after 7 days.
- `forecasts`: site_id, kind (pv|load|outage|critical_load), belief_time, target_time, horizon_min, q05..q95 (outage: p_down, p_start, p_end, expected_remaining_min), model_stage, model_version, nwp_run_time, nwp_source, flags (stale_nwp, provider_fallback, censoring_warning). Primary key (site_id, kind, belief_time, target_time). Persist the full 48-h vector at each hourly NWP refresh and at every 15-min issue only the first 6 h (24 slots); that is ~7,000 rows/site/day, ~7 M rows/day at 1,000 sites, fine for TimescaleDB with compression (retention: raw 2 years, aggregates forever). Every belief time is kept: this is what makes lead-time scoring and honest backtests possible (the OpenSTEF `available_at` discipline).
- `forecast_scores`: site_id, kind, date, horizon_class, regime, metric, value, baseline_value.
- `outage_schedule_versions`: feeder_id, fetched_at, source (ccms|ke_pdf|manual), slot vector, hash.
- Serving to the optimiser: in-process function `get_forecast_bundle(site_id, belief_time=None, horizon_slots=192)` returning a dataclass with numpy arrays (pv_q[7,192], load_q[7,192], critical_load[192], p_down[192], outage_scenarios[20,192], clear_sky_ceiling[192], metadata) and the same shape over HTTP `GET /api/v1/sites/{id}/forecasts/latest` for the UI and Product 2. The optimiser never calls providers; it reads the latest issued bundle, and refuses to plan on a bundle whose belief_time is older than 30 minutes (it then triggers forecast_issue and uses the fallback chain).
- Model registry: model_version = {site_id or fleet, kind, stage, trained_at, train_window, feature_list_hash, metrics}; artefacts as xgboost JSON in object storage; promotion rules in code, not by hand.

## 8. Fallback when a provider is down
Ordered chain, each step sets a flag in the forecast row and widens quantiles (multiply the q05-q95 half-width by 1.25 per step, capped at 2x):
1. Last cached NWP run for the cell (valid up to 48-72 h ahead): use as-is for up to 6 h of provider outage.
2. Alternate model endpoint on the same provider (ecmwf_ifs025 vs icon vs gfs): covers single-model feed failures (frequent) but not an API outage.
3. Secondary provider: forecast.solar Public (hourly PV, keyless, 12 calls/h/IP, pilot-only scale) for PV; for 1,000 sites, a self-hosted NOAA GFS GRIB puller (NOMADS, free, no key, 0.25 deg, 4 runs/day) kept warm as the independent second source: UNVERIFIED cost of operating it, estimate one small VM.
4. Provider-free models, trained in parallel and always available: PV = clear-sky x persisted clear-sky index (last 3 days, same hour, median) with Stage-1 capacity; load = the lag-only GBM variant (trained without weather features) or equivalent-day persistence; outage = the hazard model does not need weather (temperature feature imputed from climatology).
5. Telemetry outage (site offline): freeze Stage-1 ratios, use NWP-only forecasts, mark load forecasts "unobserved"; after 24 h offline the optimiser falls back to the inverter's own mode (plan expiry, guideline line 446).
Monitoring: provider health probe every 5 minutes, circuit breaker opens after 3 consecutive failures, alerts to on-call; dashboards show the share of forecasts issued on each fallback step.
