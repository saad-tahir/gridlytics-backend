# Product 2: Vendor-Agnostic PV Inverter Controller — Research Report

**Prepared for OnyxLeaf (Rawalpindi/Islamabad) — 30 October 2026.** Research performed by web search and direct fetches of the cited pages. Every claim carries a link to the page opened; where a number or fact could not be confirmed from a fetched page it is explicitly marked **not verified**.

## Scope

This report covers the edge-layer product only: a controller that reads, normalizes, and (where safely possible) controls any brand of PV inverter and BESS, exposing one uniform interface upward. The meeting notes define the product as: each inverter brand exposes current, voltage, active and reactive power over serial with "its own pattern" (Sungrow one pattern, Growatt another); the product should "detect all patterns and adapt accordingly", counter vendor lock-in ("buy inverter of my company"), fix the OEM supplier's wrong battery data, and be either "a controller (reading AND control for any brand's hardware) OR a frontend+backend", with the compatibility claim "works with all systems" and BESS included.

Our reading: the controller and the frontend/backend are not alternatives — they are two halves of one product. The **edge controller** is a small box that sits on the inverter's RS485/CAN/USB ports, speaks each brand's dialect, and normalizes everything into one data model; the **backend + frontend** is the cloud that aggregates fleets of these boxes and gives installers/owners a single pane of glass across mixed-brand sites. The interface exposed upward to Product 1 (the TOU-aware EMS/optimizer, out of scope here) is: **normalized telemetry** (PV power, grid import/export, load, battery V/I/SoC/temperature, per-phase AC quantities, alarms) plus **writable normalized setpoints** (charge/discharge power or current limits, time-of-use windows, work mode, export limit), with the controller translating each setpoint into the brand-specific register writes or serial commands and enforcing fail-safes. Optimization logic, tariff awareness, and scheduling are explicitly out of scope — this product's job is to make any inverter look like the same inverter.

## Refined problem statement

Pakistani solar sites increasingly mix inverter brands (a Growatt string inverter beside an Inverex/Voltronic hybrid beside a Deye-based unit), and each brand ships its own protocol, register layout, Wi-Fi dongle, and cloud app, so an owner or installer with N brands needs N monitoring apps and has no single point of control. Vendors exploit this: the sanctioned path to monitoring and control is their own dongle and cloud, which locks the customer into the vendor's batteries and accessories, and the dongle physically occupies the one RS485/USB port a third-party controller would need. OEM-rebadged LFP battery packs frequently report wrong state-of-charge to the inverter and to the user, so decisions (and warranties) rest on bad data. The falsifiable claims behind the product: (1) ≥80% of the inverters installed in Pakistan speak one of fewer than ~10 protocol families, almost all already reverse-engineered in open source; (2) a curated driver library plus automatic device identification can connect to an unknown site in under 15 minutes without a laptop-wielding protocol engineer; (3) enough of the common brands expose writable control registers that a cross-brand control product (not just monitoring) is feasible. Each of these is testable on a bench and in a 5-site pilot, and claim (3) is the kill test (see §Pricing, risks and kill criteria).

## The protocol problem

The point of this section: there is no single "serial pattern" to detect — there are roughly three protocol families (standard Modbus with per-vendor register maps, vendor-framed Modbus wrappers, and the Voltronic ASCII command protocol), and the register maps come overwhelmingly from community reverse engineering plus semi-public vendor PDFs.

One grounding paragraph on Modbus itself: Modbus is a 1979-era master/slave protocol where a client reads/writes numbered 16-bit registers using function codes (03 read holding registers, 04 read input registers, 06/16 write). Modbus RTU runs over RS485 serial; Modbus TCP wraps the same PDU in TCP port 502. The protocol standardizes only the envelope — **which register means what is entirely vendor-defined**, which is exactly the "each company has its own pattern" problem from the notes. SunSpec (next section) is the attempt to standardize the register semantics.

Two structural observations. First, the "pattern" differences are mostly **register-map differences over the same Modbus transport**, plus two genuinely different protocols (Voltronic ASCII; vendor dongle framings like Solarman V5, Growatt Shine, LXP). Second, the dongle problem from the notes is real and specific: the vendor Wi-Fi stick occupies the physical port. The escape hatch is that the dominant stick family — Solarman/IGEN loggers used by Deye, Solis, Sofar, and others — accepts Modbus RTU frames over its own LAN protocol: [pysolarmanv5](https://github.com/jmccrohan/pysolarmanv5) documents the proprietary Solarman V5 framing on TCP port 8899 and sends arbitrary Modbus RTU requests through the stick, so the controller can piggyback on the dongle instead of displacing it. Similarly, [grott](https://github.com/johanmeijer/grott) sits as a man-in-the-middle between a Growatt ShineWiFi stick and Growatt's cloud, parsing (and in beta, writing) without touching the serial port.

## Standards

The point: SunSpec Modbus is the only serious cross-vendor register standard, but the Asian residential/hybrid brands that dominate Pakistan largely ignore it, so a product cannot rely on it — it can only borrow its data model.

[SunSpec Modbus](https://sunspec.org/specifications/) standardizes "information models" — fixed blocks of registers with defined meanings — discovered by scanning for the "SunS" marker at base addresses 0, 40000 or 50000, as implemented in the Alliance's own [pysunspec2](https://github.com/sunspec/pysunspec2) Python library (Apache-2.0, 74 stars on the fetched page; models in SMDX/JSON; `scan()` then read/write Points). The newer [700-series DER models were approved in 2021](https://sunspec.org/sunspec-modbus-700-series-der-information-models-are-now-approved/) to carry IEEE 1547-2018 functions (volt-var, freq-watt, limit-watt). SunSpec matters in North America because [IEEE 1547-2018 requires inverters to support one of SunSpec Modbus, IEEE 2030.5, or IEEE 1815, as explained by Solar Builder](https://solarbuildermag.com/news/get-on-the-modbus-an-explanation-of-ieee-1547-and-why-it-matters-for-solar-installers/); California's Rule 21/CSIP stack uses IEEE 2030.5. SunSpec maintains a [product certification registry](https://sunspec.org/project/alpha-ess/) (per-vendor pages; Alpha ESS shown; a full machine-readable list was not fetchable in this pass), and vendors like SMA publish [SunSpec Modbus profiles](https://files.sma.de/downloads/SunSpecModbus-TI-en-11.pdf). Some Chinese vendors are members — [Sofar is a SunSpec contributing member](https://sunspec.org/contributing-members/shenzhen-sofarsolar-co-ltd/) — but membership is not implementation.

Why the Pakistani mix doesn't comply: the US/EU interconnection rules that force compliance don't apply in Pakistan; the hybrid/off-grid segment that dominates there evolved from UPS lineage (Voltronic's ASCII protocol predates SunSpec adoption in that class); and vendors have a commercial incentive to keep customers inside their dongle+cloud ecosystem. Practical consequence for OnyxLeaf: **treat SunSpec's data model (models 103/802/7xx etc.) as the internal normalized schema**, map every proprietary device onto it, and get SunSpec-compliant devices nearly free — but never assume a field device speaks it.

it is AGPL-3.0, so reuse means ideas, not code, unless OnyxLeaf open-sources that component.

**License/reuse analysis.** For a commercial embedded controller + proprietary cloud: (a) **MIT/Apache code is freely reusable** with attribution — that covers mpp-solar, pysolarmanv5, davidrapan/ha-solarman, mkaiser's YAML, kbialek (Apache-2.0), wills106 (Apache-2.0), evcc (MIT), modbus4mqtt. (b) **GPL-3.0 (SunGather)**: shipping it on the box obliges releasing the combined work's source to customers; avoid linking, reimplement from the underlying Sungrow PDF instead. (c) **AGPL-3.0 (wlcrs/huawei_solar, dbus-serialbattery, OpenEMS UI)**: AGPL's network clause makes it toxic for a closed cloud; use as documentation of register semantics, write clean implementations. (d) **OpenEMS Edge/Backend is EPL-2.0**, which is file-level copyleft and generally compatible with commercial use if modifications to EPL files are published — viable if OnyxLeaf wanted to build *on* OpenEMS rather than beside it. (e) Critically, **register maps as facts are not copyrightable in most jurisdictions, but the YAML files expressing them are**; Apache/MIT-licensed YAML profiles can be imported wholesale with attribution, which bootstraps OnyxLeaf's driver library on day one. Roughly: read-drivers for every target brand and write-drivers for 5–6 brands already exist under permissive licenses; OnyxLeaf's net-new work is the unified schema, the identification layer, the fail-safe control layer, the Voltronic-rebadge QA matrix for Pakistan, and productization.

## Pattern detection feasibility

Verdict first: **"detect all patterns and adapt" is feasible — but as curated-library fingerprinting, not as ML or automatic protocol inference.** No shipping multi-brand product learns protocols; all of them select from a human-built driver library.

(a) How existing tools actually do it. [SolarAssistant](https://solar-assistant.io/) supports ~16 brand families and "many rebranded Voltronic or Deye inverters" via a compatibility guide — i.e., a curated driver set with user/auto selection. [SunGather](https://github.com/bohdan-s/SunGather) "auto-detects the inverter model on first connection and selects the matching registers" — it reads Sungrow's device-type register and picks a known map. [dbus-serialbattery](https://github.com/mr-manuel/venus-os_dbus-serialbattery) "tries each known Battery implementation's connection test and keeps the one that succeeds" — brute-force probing over a driver library. [ha-solarman](https://github.com/davidrapan/ha-solarman) autodiscovers the logger on the LAN and selects a YAML profile. [evcc](https://github.com/evcc-io/evcc) and [OpenEMS](https://github.com/OpenEMS/openems) make the installer pick a template/component, then validate. The pattern is universal: **transport probe → identity read → map selection → validation.**

(b) Automatic protocol reverse engineering is a research field, not a product technique. [Netzob](https://github.com/AMOSSYS/Netzob) ("Protocol Reverse Engineering, Modeling and Fuzzing", per [its PyPI description](https://pypi.org/project/netzob)) infers message formats from captured traffic by sequence alignment and clustering — useful offline, in the lab, to accelerate a human reverse-engineering a new dongle protocol. It cannot tell you that register 40087 is "battery SoC in 0.1%" vs "grid frequency in 0.01 Hz"; semantics require a human with the device and a ground truth. For *control*, automatic inference is disqualifying: a mis-inferred write register on a 10 kW hybrid is a fire/warranty/legal event. The honest line for investors: ML cannot conjure register semantics; humans curate maps, software selects them.

(c) The realistic method, concretely:
1. **Transport scan**: enumerate serial ports and LAN (Solarman port 8899 scan — pysolarmanv5 ships a network scanner; Modbus TCP 502; Growatt Shine). Try baud rates 9600/115200, Modbus slave IDs 1–5, and Voltronic `QPI`/`QPIGS` probes.
2. **Identity read**: each family has cheap identity probes — Sungrow device-type code register, Deye/Solis serial-number registers via Solarman, Voltronic `QPI` returns the protocol ID (`PI30` etc.), SunSpec "SunS" scan at 0/40000/50000 ([pysunspec2](https://github.com/sunspec/pysunspec2) behavior).
3. **Map scoring**: load candidate YAML maps for that family; read 15–30 registers; score each map by plausibility constraints (voltage 150–280 V AC, frequency 49.5–50.5 Hz, SoC 0–100%, power ≤ rated). Accept the best map above a threshold; otherwise flag "unknown device" and upload a raw register dump for human curation. This scoring step is the only legitimate "pattern detection" automation, and it is a weekend of code, not a research program.
4. **Declarative register-map DSL**: every device is a YAML file (registers, scaling, enums, write points with min/max/step and safety class), versioned in a repo, hot-loadable — exactly the [Solarman `inverter_definitions`](https://github.com/StephanJoubert/home_assistant_solarman) / [evcc templates](https://github.com/evcc-io/evcc) model. New devices become data, shippable OTA without firmware releases.
5. **Conformance tests**: every map ships with recorded request/response fixtures and a bench checklist; write points are gated until bench-verified on real hardware.

What ML can honestly add: anomaly detection on normalized telemetry (drifting SoC, clipping, failing fans), fleet-level fault classification, and perhaps assisted map curation (clustering raw dumps from unknown devices against known families). That plays to OnyxLeaf's Boxlytics "predict and optimise" DNA — but it sits on top of the data layer, not inside protocol handling.

## Battery data

The wrong-SoC problem is real and structural: low-cost rebadged LFP packs ship BMS firmware whose SoC is naive coulomb counting that drifts (LFP's flat open-circuit-voltage curve between ~20–80% SoC makes voltage-based correction hard, so cheap BMSs rarely recalibrate except at full charge), and the inverter blindly displays whatever the BMS reports over the Pylontech-compatible protocol. Community tooling confirms both halves: the Pylontech RS485 console protocol has been publicly dissected (e.g. the [EEVblog "Pylontech sc0500 protocol hacking" thread](https://eevblog.com/forum/programming/pylontech-sc0500-protocol-hacking/msg4912399)), "Pylontech CAN" has become the lingua franca batteries use to talk to hybrid inverters — to the point that [ArminJo/JK-BMSToPylontechCAN](https://github.com/ArminJo/JK-BMSToPylontechCAN) exists purely to make a JK BMS *impersonate* a Pylontech on CAN so any inverter accepts it — and [dbus-serialbattery](https://github.com/mr-manuel/venus-os_dbus-serialbattery) exists largely because stock BMS data is not good enough for Victron systems, publishing its own charge/discharge limits to steer the inverter.

Estimator approach for OnyxLeaf: the controller already logs pack voltage, current and temperature at 1–10 s resolution from both the inverter and (where reachable) the BMS directly (RS485/CAN; JK via [syssi/esphome-jk-bms](https://github.com/syssi/esphome-jk-bms)-style components). On that stream, run a software SoC estimator: coulomb counting as the fast integrator, OCV-based correction applied only in the usable OCV regions (rest periods, and the curve's steep ends), fused by an extended Kalman filter; the method triad (OCV vs coulomb counting vs Kalman) is standard BMS practice as summarized in e.g. [Sunlith's SOC estimation overview](https://sunlithenergy.com/?p=4855), with the EKF literature represented by open-access treatments such as [this Energies 2022 EKF SoC paper](https://doaj.org/article/49726183f3644fee90b8859263c806f4). [PyBaMM](https://pypi.org/project/netzob) — correction: PyBaMM was **not fetched** in this pass; cite it as the standard open-source battery modeling framework (**not verified** here) — can generate OCV/parameter priors per cell chemistry offline. Realistic accuracy with V/I/T only and periodic full-charge resets is ±3–5% SoC mid-range for LFP (**engineering estimate, not a verified benchmark**) — which is a dramatic improvement over packs that display 60% and then cut off, and also yields SoH trending (capacity fade from charge-throughput between full-charge events) as a differentiating feature. The product displays *both* numbers ("BMS says 72%, estimated 58%") to build installer trust rather than silently overriding.

## Control-write risk

The point: write access is the product's differentiator and its biggest liability; evidence says control is possible on the major brands but must be engineered as a safety system, not a feature flag.

Findings. Vendors differ sharply in how sanctioned third-party control is: **Huawei** gates write access behind "elevate permissions" with installer-account credentials, after which forced battery charge/discharge and export control work ([wlcrs/huawei_solar](https://github.com/wlcrs/huawei_solar/)) — i.e., officially contemplated but credentialed. **Sungrow** publishes its hybrid register map including EMS-mode and charge/discharge registers, which the [mkaiser YAML package](https://github.com/mkaiser/Sungrow-SHx-Inverter-Modbus-Home-Assistant) drives in production in thousands of homes (708★), with the maintainer still flagging untested functions. **Deye** writes are community-routine but explicitly at-your-own-risk: [kbialek's README warns invalid values "may damage the inverter"](https://github.com/kbialek/deye-inverter-mqtt), and forum threads like powerforum's ["how to safely change settings on a Deye inverter"](https://powerforum.co.za/topic/13795-how-to-safely-change-settings-deye-inverter/) and German threads on [Deye 12K/20K active-power setpoints via Modbus](https://akkudoktor.net/t/deye-12k-und-deye-20k-wirkleistungsvorgabe-ueber-modbus-dynamischer-einspeisetarif/29759) show both demand and nervousness. **Voltronic** setting commands are long-established (parameter-setting threads such as [Axpert MKSII settings via protocol](https://powerforum.co.za/topic/22429-setting-parameters-14and-16-on-voltronic-axpert-mksii-5k-48v/)), and SolarAssistant commercially ships remote settings adjustment on these families ([solar-assistant.io](https://solar-assistant.io/)). We did not find (in this pass) a documented case of a warranty formally voided for Modbus writes — absence of evidence, **not verified** either way — but vendor terms generally reserve that right, and grott shipping a [`blockcmd` feature specifically to block remote configure/reboot commands](https://github.com/johanmeijer/grott) shows the community treats stray writes as a real hazard. A further practical risk from the Huawei page: Modbus often allows **one client connection at a time**, so the controller must own the port and multiplex all consumers.

Fail-safe design (to put in the spec): (1) **write allow-list per device map** — only bench-verified registers, with min/max/step/rate limits encoded in the YAML, everything else read-only; (2) **watchdog/dead-man pattern** — every external setpoint has a TTL; on controller crash, comms loss, or cloud loss, the inverter is reverted to its own standalone mode (self-consumption/TOU as configured locally), mirroring how [dbus-serialbattery steers via published limits the inverter can ignore](https://github.com/mr-manuel/venus-os_dbus-serialbattery) rather than seizing control; (3) **read-back verification** after every write, alarm on mismatch; (4) **two-tier product**: monitoring-only mode by default, control unlocked per-site after a conformance check, with per-brand legal language; (5) **command audit log** shipped to cloud for dispute defense.

## Product scope and methodology

The point: build a thin, boring, data-driven edge stack whose entire brand knowledge lives in versioned YAML maps, and spend the engineering budget on identification, fail-safety, and the bench.

**Driver abstraction.** Internal normalized model = a SunSpec-shaped subset (inverter AC/DC telemetry ≈ models 101–103, storage ≈ 802, controls ≈ 7xx semantics) implemented as typed channels, borrowing OpenEMS's Nature/Channel idea ([OpenEMS](https://github.com/OpenEMS/openems)) without the Java stack — Go or Python on Linux. Three transport plugins (Modbus RTU/TCP incl. [Solarman V5 framing](https://github.com/jmccrohan/pysolarmanv5); Voltronic ASCII per [mpp-solar](https://github.com/jblance/mpp-solar); vendor-cloud MITM per [grott](https://github.com/johanmeijer/grott)) + CAN for batteries. On top, a **register-map DSL in YAML**: device identity probes, read blocks with scaling/enums, write points with safety metadata — seeded by converting the Apache/MIT-licensed community maps ([ha-solarman definitions](https://github.com/StephanJoubert/home_assistant_solarman), [mkaiser Sungrow YAML](https://github.com/mkaiser/Sungrow-SHx-Inverter-Modbus-Home-Assistant), [kbialek Deye docs](https://github.com/kbialek/deye-inverter-mqtt), [wills106 plugins](https://github.com/wills106/homeassistant-solax-modbus)) with attribution. **Probe-and-identify flow** as specified in §Pattern detection: transport scan → identity read → candidate-map scoring with plausibility constraints → accept/flag. **Conformance rig**: a bench with one real unit of each target brand; every map version must pass recorded-fixture replay + live read sanity + (for write points) a scripted write/read-back/revert cycle on the bench before it can be enabled in the field; unknown field devices upload raw dumps that become new maps centrally.

**Build order for Pakistan (first 5 driver families):** 1) **Voltronic/Axpert family** (covers Inverex-class local rebadges — the volume segment; MIT-licensed reference in mpp-solar); 2) **Deye family** (incl. Sunsynk rebadges; strong write support precedent); 3) **Growatt** (serial Modbus + Shine MITM); 4) **Sungrow** (C&I credibility; official PDF + proven control YAML); 5) **Solis via Solarman** (fresh [300 MW PK distribution](https://solarquarter.com/2024/03/25/solis-embarks-on-a-sustainable-venture-with-ae-power-with-300mw-distribution-deal-to-transform-pakistans-energy-sector/)), with the Solarman V5 transport also unlocking Sofar/others nearly free. Battery side in parallel: Pylontech-CAN-compatible reading (the de-facto standard) + JK BMS, then the SoC estimator.

**Edge/cloud split.** Edge box (CM4-class, 2× isolated RS485 + CAN, eMMC, DC-input PSU off the battery bus like [SolarAssistant's option A](https://solar-assistant.io/shop/products/device_rpi_zero)): all protocol work, local buffering for Pakistan's connectivity gaps, local web UI, watchdog-governed control. Cloud: fleet dashboard, map distribution/OTA, alerting, the normalized API that Product 1 (EMS/optimizer) consumes, audit logs. The controller must work indefinitely offline — local-first is both a reliability requirement and the anti-lock-in marketing story.

**Pricing anchors.** SolarAssistant: [one-time perpetual per-device license](https://solar-assistant.io/help/updates/terms_update_oct_2024), [$149 hardware+software bundle](https://solar-assistant.io/shop/products/device_rpi_zero). **Risks:** (1) vendor dongle/firmware updates breaking MITM and logger piggybacks (mitigate: direct-RS485 install option always available); (2) GPL/AGPL contamination if community code is vendored carelessly (mitigate: license audit gate in CI; reimplement from PDFs where needed); (3) SolarAssistant entering Pakistan or local clones undercutting (mitigate: control + battery-truth + Urdu/installer channel as moats); (4) damage/warranty liability from writes (mitigate: §Control-write fail-safes, insurance, per-brand disclaimers); (5) the channel partners being installers who want margin on hardware, not software partners (unknown until verified); (6) grey-market model churn: Pakistani rebadges change OEM internals without changing the shell, so identification must trust protocol probes, never the logo.

## Saudi Arabia and GCC

The point: the GCC is a different product on the same platform — tier-1 brands (Huawei/Sungrow/SMA class) with real Modbus/SunSpec support replace the Voltronic rebadge problem, but regulated equipment lists, SASO/DEWA certification, and accredited-installer regimes make it a year-2 market best entered through ESCO/O&M and hybrid-plant partners rather than direct retail.

**Brand mix.** KSA's solar inverter market is led by global tier-1 vendors: a ResearchAndMarkets outlook names [Fronius, Sungrow, SMA, Schneider Electric, ABB and Huawei Solar Arabia as key players in Saudi Arabia](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220819005272/en/Saudi-Arabia-Solar-Inverter-Market-Outlook-Report-2022-2028-Key-Players-are-Fronius-Intl-Sungrow-Power-Supply-SMA-Solar-Technology-Schneider-Electric-ABB-Huawei-Solar-Arabia-Co.---ResearchAndMarkets.com) (2022–2028 report; exact shares behind paywall, **not verified**), consistent with [Huawei and Sungrow leading Wood Mackenzie's global inverter ranking for H1 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2026/01/05/huawei-and-sungrow-lead-global-solar-inverter-rankings-in-h1-2025-wood-mackenzie/amp/). Dubai's eligible-equipment regime has historically listed European/tier-1 units — [Ingeteam](https://www.pv-magazine.com/press-releases/ingeteam-string-inveters-qualifies-for-dewa_100022548/), [REFUsol](https://www.pveurope.eu/inverter/refusol-pv-inverters-listed-dubai-electricity-authority), [Schneider Electric](https://solarbuildermag.com/news/schneider-electric-receives-inverter-approval-from-dubai-authority/) and more recently [Solplanet](https://mesia.com/?p=3975) all announced DEWA listing. Voltronic-family work is irrelevant in GCC grid-tied segments.

**Regulation and certification.** UAE/Dubai: DEWA's Shams Dubai programme requires both panels and inverters to appear on [DEWA's approved equipment list (150+ components, via the Hab Reeh platform), with inverters certified to IEC 62109-1/-2 and IEC 62116 anti-islanding, and every application submitted by a DEWA-accredited consultant/contractor (111 active in 2025)](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/uae/guides/dewa-shams-dubai); DEWA's own [eligible-equipment page](https://www.dewa.gov.ae/en/builder/shams-dubai-guides-tools/drrg/eligible-equipment-list) returned an error when fetched (list contents **not verified** directly), and an older [2017 DRRG equipment list PDF exists on dewa.gov.ae](https://www.dewa.gov.ae/~/media/DRRG%20equipment_list%203Augt2017%20-%20Temporary%20Derrogation.ashx) (not opened). The fetched guide states **no monitoring/communication interface mandate** — no SunSpec/IEC 61850 requirement appears for small-scale DRRG systems — and DEWA simply installs a bidirectional smart meter. KSA: the [ECRA Regulatory Framework for Small-Scale Solar PV (ERD-TA-012)](https://rise.esmap.org/data/files/library/saudi-arabia/Renewable%20Energy/Kingdom%20of%20Saudi%20Arabia_Regulatory%20Framework%20for%20Small-%20Scale%20Solar%20PV%20Systems.pdf) covers 1 kW–2 MW systems, requires "material compliance with Saudi standards or equivalent international standards" with the DSP and SASO maintaining an approved-components list, mandates certified installers, requires a separate generation meter only above 100 kW, and **contains no provision for remote/third-party control and no communication-interface specification** (ECRA has since been renamed; current SERA-era rules **not verified** this pass).
