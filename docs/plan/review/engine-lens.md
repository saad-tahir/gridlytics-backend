## Part 4. The optimiser (lines 277 to 399)

### E1. The reserve rule fires inside outages it does not need to cover, and prices the shortfall as if the outage were certain, once per step
- Severity: high
- Location: Part 4.3 "Reserve (soft)" (lines 319 to 325); Part 4.4 objective term `c_reserve sum_t r[t]` and "c_reserve defaults to the genset's marginal cost" (line 363); OPT-04 (line 996).
- Claim: `E[t] + r[t] >= soc_min E_cap + R[t]` with `R[t] > 0 when p_out[t] >= p_threshold`, slack `r[t]` priced at the genset's marginal cost; "the solver prefers to fill the reserve ... but will not pay more than the diesel it saves".
- Defect. (a) `p_out[t]` is P(down) at step t (Part 5.3), which is 1.0 inside an observed outage and inside every scheduled window, so the reserve row is active at every step of the outage, where the balance row already forces Lc to be served from PV, battery, genset or U. If the genset serves Lc the objective pays diesel in the balance and again `c_reserve` on the slack the genset made unnecessary: one kWh priced twice. Without a genset, U is priced at VoLL and the slack again at `c_reserve`. (b) The slack is charged at the full diesel marginal cost (Rs 110 to 142 per kWh) however small the outage probability above the 0.2 threshold; the expected cost of a 1 kWh shortfall at p = 0.2 is about Rs 28, below the Rs 35.68 earned by discharging into the peak, so the rule makes the opposite call to the report's own arithmetic (p x 142.2 against 35.68) for every p between 0.2 and about 0.3, and that call is the "hold_for_outage" increment the pilot measures. (c) The slack is summed over every step the shortfall persists, so a 10 kWh shortfall held for 8 steps before a forecast outage costs 8 times what one outage start could cost. (d) `c_reserve` has no default at sites without a genset. (e) `Lc_hi` in the R[t] formula is not an input of 4.1 (Lc is already a 75th percentile per 5.2).
- Evidence: R3 section 3.5 recommends the reserve-SoC rule for v1 and, of the scenario form, says "use the expected-value (sum over tz weighted by p_out[tz]) ... minimax is REopt's planning stance, not a dispatch stance"; both prototypes price the reserve only at steps ahead of a known window (`R1-target-milp-prototype.py` lines 91 to 102, "energy needed to carry critical load through the NEXT outage window, from each step"; `milp_bench.py` line 157, "before each outage window"), never inside one; the report's "Which part is the optimiser's increment" values holding reserve at 142.2 minus 35.68 only for a realised outage.
- Replacement text for lines 319 to 325 and 363: "Reserve (soft, expected value): for every step t with g[t] = 1, `E[t] + r[t] >= soc_min E_cap + R[t]`, `r[t] >= 0`, where R[t] is the energy to carry Lc through an outage that starts at t+1 and lasts d(t) steps: `R[t] = min((soc_max - soc_min) E_cap, sum_{k=t+1..t+d(t)} max(0, Lc[k] - PVlo[k]) h / eta_d) + 2 sigma0_frac E_cap`. No reserve row is generated for steps with g[t] = 0 (the balance row governs there). The slack is priced per step at the probability that an outage starts in the next step times the cost of being short: `c_reserve[t] = p_start[t] x c_short`, with `c_short` the genset's marginal Rs/kWh at a genset site and VoLL otherwise; p_start comes from the outage forecaster's hazard chain (5.3); rows with p_start[t] below 0.01 are dropped. A manual reserve override enters as a floor `E[t] >= R_manual` with its own slack at VoLL."

### E2. The ICP is modelled as a time-ordered "exhaustion step", but the bill prices by monthly totals, so the single price vector is wrong before t* on every month that ends above baseline
- Severity: high
- Location: Part 4.3 "ICP" (line 343); Part 6.1 ICP row ("the marginal price in each period whose baseline is exhausted"); Part 6.4 ("the ICP marginal price for each period whose baseline is exhausted"); TAR-03 (line 919), TAR-07 (line 923), OPT-06 (line 998).
- Claim: the tariff engine "computes, per period, the step t* at which that period's baseline is exhausted from month-to-date metering and the load forecast, and hands the MILP one price vector: the period rate before t*, the flat incremental price ... after it, so the TOU spread collapses for incremental units exactly as the bill will show".
- Defect: the bill (R9 section 13 steps 4 and 5; Part 6.3 step 4) splits the month's kWh per period into baseline and incremental by totals, `incremental_p = max(0, E_imp[p] - baseline_p)`; order inside the month is irrelevant. If the month's total in period p ends above baseline_p, the derivative of the bill with respect to any kWh in that period, at any time in the month, is the ICP price; if it ends below, the period rate everywhere. "Before t*" has no meaning for a marginal decision. On an ICP-eligible site (consumption 25 to 35 percent above baseline per R9 section 5, which is why the package exists) the engine would see the full Rs 12.85 spread from day 1 to day t* of every month and cycle the battery for an arbitrage the bill never pays, losing Rs 10.8 per kWh of wear. The "exact chain behind a flag" inherits the error: both prototypes count the baseline only against imports inside the 48-hour horizon (R1 line 111, "baseline remaining this month (kWh); small so the binary is exercised"; `milp_bench.py` line 52, "baseline consumption allowance left in this period, kWh (scaled to the horizon)") and ignore the rest-of-month consumption that decides the split. The plan gives no rule for when the flag is on, and computes t* "from the load forecast" rather than forecast net imports (load minus PV minus battery), which puts t* too early at every PV site.
- Evidence: R9 section 5 ("Rs 22.98 per kWh on peak and off-peak incremental consumption over the corresponding reference period"; "the bill rebuild must therefore split kWh into (baseline, incremental) by period before pricing"); R9 section 13 steps 4 and 5; R1 section 12 reading 2 and R7 section 1.3 reading 4 describe a horizon-local baseline counter.
- Replacement text for line 343: "ICP (R9 sections 5 and 13): the ICP prices each period's monthly total: units above `baseline_p` cost the ICP price (Rs 22.98 plus positive FCA plus the energy-scaling duty and tax share, without QTA and the surcharge), units below cost the period rate. The marginal price of a horizon step is a property of where the month will end, not of the step's position. The tariff engine computes per period `Q_p = month-to-date imports + forecast net imports (load minus PV minus the previous plan's battery flow) for the rest of the month, inside and beyond the horizon`. If `Q_p - baseline_p` exceeds the horizon's largest possible swing (`G_max x steps in p x h`), every horizon step of period p is priced at the ICP price; if `baseline_p - Q_p` exceeds it, at the period rate; otherwise the model carries one binary per period and month, `b_p`, with `Z_p >= Q_rest_p + sum_{t in p} h G[t] - baseline_p`, `Z_p <= M b_p`, `Z_p <= Q_rest_p + sum_{t in p} h G[t] - baseline_p + M (1 - b_p)`, `Z_p >= 0`, and the energy term `rate_p (sum_{t in p} h G[t] - Z_p) + c_icp Z_p` (Q_rest_p is a constant). Two binaries at most, no chain. TAR-03 and TAR-07 accept on a golden where the peak period's month ends above baseline and the off-peak's does not, and on the uncertain middle case."

### E3. "A plan is never infeasible" is false: the genset block has hard rows that contradict each other and the carried state
- Severity: high
- Location: Part 4.3 genset block (lines 327 to 339): `u[t] <= 1 - g[t]`, the min-up and min-down windows "clipped at T", `sum_t h u[t] <= cap_hours_today - hours_run_today`, `sum_t F[t] <= fuel_available`, and "The elapsed on or off steps from the previous tick enter as fixed values of u" (line 339); Part 4.5 result policy (line 371: "A plan is never 'infeasible' because reserve, lost load and caps are soft rows ... the two contradictory inputs the API refuses with 422 are an SoC outside the usable band (handled by a one-step recovery row) and a genset minimum load above its rating"); Part 4.8 ("a genset hold fixes u = 0").
- Claim: only two contradictory inputs exist and the API refuses them.
- Defect: (a) genset on at the tick with elapsed on-steps below UT, grid back (g[0] = 1, or a scheduled return inside UT), `parallel_allowed = false`: the fixings force u = 1 while `u[t] <= 1 - g[t]` forces u = 0. This is every outage end during the minimum up time, the most common genset event at a load-shedding site. (b) Fixings u = 1 with the daily run-hour cap already reached, or with `fuel_available = 0` (`F[t] = h b S u[t] > 0`): infeasible. (c) A genset-hold override (u = 0) against the same fixings: infeasible. (d) Windows "clipped at T" with the right-hand side still `UT v[t]` or `DT (u[t-1] - u[t])` cannot be met when fewer than UT or DT steps remain, so no start is possible in the last UT-1 steps and a forced stop (grid return) in the last DT-1 steps is infeasible. (e) The 422 sentence contradicts itself (refused, yet "handled by a recovery row"); an SoC outside the band is routine after a native-mode day or a BMS reset and must be handled, not refused. The relaxed retry (min up and down dropped, 24-hour horizon) hides (a) to (d) at the price of a failed solve plus a degraded 24-hour plan every time, and the builder, told the cases cannot arise, writes no mechanism test for them.
- Evidence: R3 section 2.6 states the initial-condition fixings for a model with no grid-exclusion row; R2 section 3.5 scales the min-on right-hand side by the clipped window ("(window_end - t) x start[t]") and handles an out-of-band soc_init with single-recovery binaries instead of refusing it; R1 section 4 lists evcc's two 400 cases for a model with no genset.
- Replacement text: "Genset hard rows are made consistent with the carried state before the build: (1) initial fixings apply only to steps where the grid is absent or parallel running is allowed (`u[t] = 1` fixed only while `g[t] = 0 or parallel_allowed`); a grid return ends the minimum up time (the ATS transfers regardless); (2) a run-hour cap, fuel cap or genset-hold override that conflicts with a fixing wins and the fixing is dropped with reason `genset_state_overridden`; (3) window rows are scaled to the clipped window: `sum_{k=t}^{min(t+UT-1, T-1)} u[k] >= (min(t+UT-1, T-1) - t + 1) v[t]`, and the mirror for DT; (4) `E0` outside `[soc_min, soc_max] E_cap` enters through EMHASS's single-recovery rows (R2 section 3.5), never a 422; (5) the API refuses only `f_min > 1`, `E_cap <= 0`, `P_ch or P_dis <= 0`, `UT or DT > T`. OPT-02's mechanism tests include each of (1) to (4) and assert `optimal` without a relaxed retry."

### E4. The terminal value sits above the all-in off-peak price plus wear, so the plan hoards grid energy at the horizon end, the opposite of the stated intent
- Severity: high
- Location: Part 4.4 "Terminal value v_end" (line 365); Part 6.4 (same rule); 4.1 Terminal row.
- Claim: "v_end ... eta_d times the expected highest price of the next day (the peak-window price, or the diesel price per kWh when the outage forecast for the next day exceeds the threshold), so the plan does not empty the battery at hour 48 and does not hoard it either."
- Defect: at B-2, v_end = 0.95 x 35.68 = Rs 33.9 per kWh before adders and taxes (about Rs 48 with them), while the all-in off-peak import is roughly (22.83 + 0.52 + 3.23 + FCA) grossed up, about Rs 32 to 34; charging costs eta_c losses and nothing else in the objective. The model therefore fills the battery from the grid in the last off-peak hours of every horizon and values every kWh at a price that ignores the Rs 10.8 wear of discharging it, the calendar cost of holding it, and the fact that the next peak absorbs at most P_dis times the window. Under the ICP (E2) the "highest price of the next day" is the ICP price, not the TOU peak, and the sentence does not say so. The bias reaches back through the shadow price of stored energy and shows up as grid charging at hours 40 to 48 of every plan.
- Evidence: both prototypes deliberately set the terminal value below the off-peak price (R1 line 84, "p_term = 20.0 ... below off-peak"; `milp_bench.py` line 76, "TERMINAL = 30.0" against an off-peak of 36); R3 section 8 proposes "the expected next-day peak price times eta_d" for an objective that at that point has no wear term; R6 section 6.8 gives the wear and calendar rupees to net off.
- Replacement text for line 365: "Terminal value: `v_end = max(0, eta_d c_next - c_cyc - c_cal_hold)` where c_next is the highest marginal import price of day 3 from the same vector the horizon uses (ICP-aware), or the genset marginal cost when day 3's outage probability exceeds p_threshold; applied only to the energy the next peak can absorb: `- v_end E_use` with `E_use <= E[T]` and `E_use <= P_dis H_peak + R_next`. v_end then sits below the all-in off-peak price by construction, so the plan neither empties nor fills the battery at hour 48. OPT-07's golden asserts no grid charging in the last 6 hours of a B-2 case with no outage forecast."

### E5. MDI rows use sliding windows the bill engine does not, ignore the open interval's elapsed half, are not grossed up like the energy price, and "checked against Lhi" is not a constraint
- Severity: medium
- Location: Part 4.3 MDI (line 341); Part 4.1 MDI row and "peak-shaving decisions are checked against Lhi" (line 299); Part 4.8 (`mdi_interval_partial` carried but unused); Part 6.3 step 3 (fixed 30-minute blocks aligned to the hour); Part 15.1 ("robust peak-shaving decisions on the upper load quantile").
- Defect: (a) "for every window of n_mdi consecutive steps" is a sliding 30-minute mean; the bill engine and R9 section 3 use fixed blocks aligned to the hour, so the optimiser's M is a different quantity from the billed MDI (conservative, but plan and counterfactual then disagree on what the peak was). (b) The first block of the horizon is already half elapsed; `site_state.mdi_interval_partial` is collected for this and never enters a row, so a plan can set 200 kW at step 0 after a 300 kW first half and believe the block mean is 200. (c) `rate_mdi M` is a pre-tax rupee while the energy price carries "the duty and tax share"; where GST applies to fixed charges (R9 section 7, configurable base) the demand term is understated by 18 percent relative to the energy term and the solver trades the two at the wrong ratio. (d) "checked against Lhi" is not an implementable instruction; a plan solved on q50 load sets a peak the real load exceeds.
- Evidence: R2 section 3.5 demand-charge epigraph ("the first row carries the already-elapsed part of the open interval"); R9 sections 3 and 13 step 3; R3 section 6.3 measurement note.
- Replacement text: "MDI: blocks follow the site's MDI setting (default fixed 30-minute blocks aligned to the hour, as the bill engine). For the open block: `M >= (G_elapsed n_elapsed + sum_{k in rest of block} G[k]) / n_mdi`, with `G_elapsed` the measured mean of the elapsed minutes; for every later block inside the billing month `M >= (1 / n_mdi) sum_{k in block} G[k]`. Robustness: the same rows are also written with `G[k] + (Lhi[k] - L[k])` in place of `G[k]`, so the peak holds under the 75th-percentile load with the planned battery flow. `rate_mdi` is grossed up by the duty and tax factor the bill engine applies to fixed charges for the site."

### E6. Scheduled load-shedding windows enter the model twice: as a certain outage (g = 0) and inside p_out, although adherence is about 80 percent
- Severity: medium
- Location: Part 4.1 Grid row ("g[t] in {0,1} availability (scheduled windows, 0 during an observed outage)"); Part 4.3 Grid (line 315); Part 2.4 step 3; Part 5.3 (`P(down) = 1 - (1 - p_sched)(1 - p_unsched)` with a Beta(8, 2) adherence prior).
- Defect: a scheduled window with 80 percent adherence is treated as certain: import and export are forced to zero, the served load drops to Lc, the genset is allowed. On the 20 percent of evenings the grid stays up, the plan has planned Lc instead of L through the peak window, the import is unplanned and the MDI rows saw none of it. The same window also raises p_out, so the reserve rows (E1) fire for an outage the balance already assumes. The forecaster computes exactly the number the model needs (p_sched = indicator x adherence) and the model ignores it.
- Evidence: Part 5.3 adherence model; R5 section 4.3 ("separate adherence for 'starts on time' and 'ends on time' ... because late restoration is the costly error").
- Replacement text for the 4.1 Grid row: "g[t] = 0 only for an observed outage (for d_exp steps) and for scheduled windows whose posterior adherence exceeds 0.9; every other scheduled window enters only through p_out and the reserve rule, and the balance serves L. The forecaster's late-restoration adherence extends d_exp by its expected overrun."

### E7. The outage balance serves the critical load whether or not the genset runs, so genset-backed plants are mis-modelled and the reserve is sized to the whole plant
- Severity: medium
- Location: Part 4.3 Balance (lines 309 to 313: "served load is L when the grid is up and the critical load when it is down; the ATS sheds the rest"); Part 5.2 (line 424: critical load = "the 75th percentile of observed load during adequately backed outages"); Part 1.1 value stream 1.
- Defect: at the first-buyer sites (textile, food, rice with a plant-sized genset) the genset carries the whole plant during load shedding; "adequately backed" outages are genset-backed, so the learned Lc equals L, R[t] becomes the whole usable capacity on every evening with p_out above 0.2, and the diesel the battery displaces is computed on Lc only. Where the ATS sheds and only the battery carries a critical subset, Lserved = Lc is right. One Lserved serves both regimes, no site setting says which applies, and nothing says which load the genset serves when it runs.
- Evidence: the report's worked example values genset displacement on the full 100 kWh at Rs 142.2; U3 section 1.2 carries `load_power_backup_w` to "separate critical load for the outage reserve rule", which the plan's balance never uses; R3 section 3.1 point 2 (REopt's switch to L_crit is the no-genset island).
- Replacement text for line 309: "Lserved[t] = g[t] L[t] + (1 - g[t]) (Lc[t] + m (L[t] - Lc[t]) u[t]), where m is the site's `outage_load_mode` (0: the ATS carries only the critical circuits on battery and genset; 1: the genset carries the whole plant and the battery alone carries Lc). The term is linear in u because L and Lc are data. Lc is learned from `load_power_backup_w` where the point exists, otherwise from outages in which the genset was off and the SoC stayed above the cut-off plus 5 points; genset-backed outages do not define Lc."

### E8. The daily run-hour cap is written over the whole 48-hour horizon against today's remaining hours
- Severity: medium
- Location: Part 4.3 (line 335: `sum_t h u[t] <= cap_hours_today - hours_run_today`).
- Defect: the sum runs over all 192 steps; when today's cap is nearly used the genset cannot run tomorrow at all, so tomorrow's outage is served by unserved load at VoLL or by hoarded battery, and tomorrow's plan is wrong on every tick of a heavy day. The fuel row has the same shape and is correct (one tank).
- Replacement text: "for each local day d inside the horizon: `sum_{t in d} h u[t] <= cap_hours - hours_run_today x [d is today]`."

### E9. Units and definitions the builder cannot resolve: sigma0, E_cap, the calendar slope, c_hot, Lc_hi
- Severity: medium
- Location: Part 4.1 Battery row (line 292: "E_cap usable, E0 (the SoC estimate times E_cap), sigma0 ... the hot-and-full penalty c_hot"); Part 4.3 Battery (`soc_min E_cap <= E[t] <= soc_max E_cap`), Reserve ("R is widened by 2 sigma0 E_cap", line 325), Wear ("a calendar holding cost b(T[t]) h E[t] ... at 95 percent SoC the cost reaches about Rs 0.13 per kWh of pack per 15-minute step at 45 degrees", line 349); Part 4.4 ("hot-and-full penalty", line 356).

(b) E_cap is "usable" and then bounded again by soc_min and soc_max: the usable window applied twice (R6 section 5.5 defines usable as nominal x SoH x (soc - 2 sigma - floor) x eta_dis). (c) The calendar term is written `b(T) h E[t]`; R6 section 6.4 gives b in Rs per kWh of pack per 15-minute step per unit SoC (0.109 at 45 C, 0.034 at 25 C), already per step, so the h = 0.25 factor cuts it four times; the figures the plan quotes (0.13, 0.04) are levels at 95 percent SoC, not slopes. (d) c_hot (4.1) and "hot-and-full penalty" (4.4) are never defined and are presumably the calendar term of 4.3. (e) Lc_hi is used in R[t] and never defined (E1).
- Evidence: R6 sections 5.3, 5.5, 6.4 and 6.5 (`sum_t b(T_t) x E x soc_t`).
- Replacement text: "Battery inputs: E_nom = nameplate x SoH (kWh); the band `soc_min E_nom <= E[t] <= soc_max E_nom`; `E0 = soc_est E_nom`; `sigma0` a fraction (points / 100); the reserve widening `2 sigma0 E_nom`. Calendar term: `sum_t b(T[t]) E[t]` with b in Rs per kWh stored per 15-minute step (R6 6.4 slopes: 0.109 at 45 C, 0.034 at 25 C, interpolated on the forecast enclosure temperature); no h factor; this is the whole of the 'hot-and-full penalty', so c_hot is deleted."

### E12. OPT-04's acceptance is a tautology

## Part 5. The forecasters (lines 401 to 476)

### E13. Every forecast and replay gate before the pilot runs on twin data generated by the models under test, so no gate can fail; the plan has no real-data gate and no owner task that supplies real 1-minute history
- Severity: high
- Location: Stage 4 exit check (line 987: "the WP5 gates on twin data"); FOR-04 (line 976, "skill against smart persistence computed on twin data"); FOR-07 (line 979, "beats persistence on twin data at every horizon class"); FOR-09 (line 981, "Brier skill against schedule-only on twin data"); VER-02 (line 961, "on twin-generated baseline days: daily import within 3 percent and throughput within 5 percent"); Part 7.4 item 3 (twin PV "from the clear-sky profile with cloud noise", load "from a template"); Part 1.4 "Forecasts ready" row (which correctly says "4 weeks of pilot data"); Part 15.2 owner tasks O3 and O4 (bills, settings, no 1-minute history).
- Defect: the twin's PV is the clear-sky model times noise, its load is the template, its outages are a hazard by hour; the PV forecaster's stage 0 is the same clear-sky chain, the load stage 0 is the same template, the outage model is the same hazard family. A skill score of the forecaster against persistence on that data measures the twin's noise generator, not the forecaster: with periodic template load, equivalent-day persistence is near-perfect and FOR-07 cannot be met; with white cloud noise, FOR-04 is met trivially. ## The three improvements that would most raise value or credibility within the same scope

1. **A marginal-price audit of the objective, written as goldens.** Every rupee coefficient the optimiser sees must equal the derivative of the bill engine's own output for that site and month: the ICP by month-end position (E2), the reserve slack at `p_start x c_short` and only where the grid is up (E1), the terminal value net of wear and capped at what the next peak absorbs (E4), MDI blocks and gross-up as billed (E5), scheduled windows by their adherence (E6), the genset balance by `outage_load_mode` (E7). One hand-worked case per value stream of Part 1.1 (reserve, PV shift, MDI, TOU, genset) in which the bill engine prices a one-kWh perturbation and the test asserts the optimiser's coefficient equals it. This is where the product's increment is won or lost, it costs about two days, and it makes the "why this plan" vocabulary (4.7) provably consistent with the bill.

2. **Honest baselines and measurement that a CFO cannot pick apart.** An immutable native capture (read-back or photograph), a product-computed "best static table" tuned baseline at every site (not only cycle charging at genset sites), a per-site metering factor fitted from real bills and applied to both arms, a block switchback with priced carry-over and stratified outage days, and fidelity as a gate rather than a multiplier (E25, E17, E24). The owner's pricing rule is one third of verified savings; these four changes are what make "verified" survive a dispute, and they add no new component.

3. **Real data before the pilot, and "increment retained" as the delivery gate.** One owner task (a month of real 1-minute data plus the bill from a partner site, due before Stage 3) so that the replay, the forecasters and the backtest get a non-circular check before the go/no-go (E13); and DLV-01 measured as the share of the optimiser's saving the compiled table keeps against the native replay, with a per-brand MDI translation so the third value stream exists in tier 2 (E19, E22). Together they turn Stage 6's go/no-go from a report about the twin into a report about a site.

## Verdict on Parts 4 to 8 as written

The plan gives the builder far more than the two reports do: a complete variable set, every row written out, a rupee-only objective with no penalty base, the carried state, the result policy, a tariff object model that matches R9 line by line, a contract that matches U3, a twin, and acceptance rows per item. That is the right shape, and most of it is right. But as written it would not give the builder what it needs for Parts 4 to 8, because the three economic terms the product's increment depends on price the wrong thing (the ICP by step order instead of month-end position, the reserve as a certain outage charged per step and inside outages, the terminal value above the off-peak price), the genset block fails on routine states the plan says cannot occur, and the gates that are supposed to catch all of this cannot fail on the data they run on (twin-generated forecasts and replays, a 5 percent objective tolerance, "or the slack priced"). An Opus build would pass every gate in Stages 3 to 7 and arrive at the pilot with an engine that cycles for arbitrage the ICP bill does not pay, hoards at hour 48, fails over to a relaxed 24-hour plan at every outage end, and a switchback the installer cannot run. Every defect above has a short replacement text and none changes the architecture, the stack or the stage order; with E1 to E4, E13, E18, E19 and E24 applied and the mediums folded into the affected items' acceptance rows, Parts 4 to 8 would be sufficient for the builder.
